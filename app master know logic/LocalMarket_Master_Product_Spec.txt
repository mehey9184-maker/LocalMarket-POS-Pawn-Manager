LOCALMARKET MASTER PRODUCT SPECIFICATION
================================================================================
Version: Master Product Specification v1.0
Date: 23 September 2026
Baseline: GitHub main commit 31ef614006d3a001abc3daaff9e363af29df771f
Repo: mehey9184-maker/LocalMarket-POS-Pawn-Manager
Platform identity: LocalMarket, powered by LocalEats

This file is the persistent product memory for LocalMarket.
Use it as the source of truth for future implementation sessions.


1. PRODUCT PURPOSE
------------------
LocalMarket is a shop operating system for second-hand retail and pawn businesses. It is designed to manage the whole shop while remaining fast enough for a busy cashier counter.

The software must help staff do their existing work faster rather than trying to replace the worker. The product should feel practical, learnable, calm, and psychologically supportive.

The shop owner should experience LocalMarket primarily as their own branded business software. LocalMarket and Powered by LocalEats should remain visible in appropriate platform/support/about areas without overpowering the shop brand.


2. CORE PRODUCT PRINCIPLE
-------------------------
REMOVE WORK, DO NOT CREATE WORK.

Every workflow should ask only for information that is necessary for that specific job. Unknown historical information is a valid state. Never force users to invent a person, price, date, source, or verification just to satisfy a form.

Use progressive disclosure: normal workers see the minimum needed to complete the task; managers see more controls; owners see business-level configuration and reporting; technical platform administration remains hidden from normal shop users.


3. CORE BUSINESS MODEL
----------------------
LocalMarket has three distinct ways stock enters the business and one main way stock leaves the business.

1) Existing Stock: the shop already owns the item. No person record is required.

2) Buy From Person: the shop buys an item from a real seller today. Seller history is retained.

3) Pawn: the shop takes an item as collateral from a real pawn customer. Customer and loan records are retained.

4) Retail Sale: the shop sells stock. A retail buyer normally does not become a persistent customer record.


4. FINAL NAVIGATION MODEL
-------------------------
Primary navigation should be: Home, Sell, Add Stock, Inventory, Customers, Profile.

Add Stock contains the three acquisition paths: Existing Stock, Buy From Person, Pawn.

Compliance, vault, staff management, reports, and advanced settings should appear inside the relevant workspaces or manager/owner areas rather than as a crowded top-level navigation bar.

The global scanner button should remain removed. Scanning is contextual to the workflow where it is useful.


5. HOME DASHBOARD
-----------------
Recommendation: make Home a daily operating dashboard rather than a dense analytics dashboard.

Owner and manager view: Sales Today, Items Sold Today, Active Pawns, Loans Due Soon/Overdue, Stock Needing Attention, Recent Activity, and quick actions.

Cashier view: greeting, Sell, Add Stock, Find Item, Find Customer, and small recent activity. Hide management-only figures such as profit and acquisition cost unless permitted by role.

Alerts should be clickable and lead directly to the relevant record. Guidance should be quiet and contextual rather than tutorial popups.


6. USER ROLES AND PERMISSIONS
-----------------------------
Expose only two shop-managed staff roles in the interface: Cashier and Manager. The technical admin role remains in the database/platform but must be hidden from normal owners and staff.

Owner is the business-account controller and should have owner-level access to shop setup, staff, reports, branding, business settings, and sensitive financial information.

Cashier: sell, search, add stock, buy from sellers, create pawns, use customer/seller lookup, and complete permitted counter operations. No unrestricted historical editing, no sensitive cost/profit visibility, and no unrestricted refunds/voids.

Manager: cashier capabilities plus approvals, refunds/voids, price changes, more reporting, and controlled operational administration.

Owner: full shop management, staff, branding, business profile, reports, and multi-branch capabilities when enabled.


7. OWNER ONBOARDING
-------------------
Onboarding must use: Basic setup -> start using app -> complete legal/business profile later.

Initial setup should request only the essentials: shop name, logo, phone/contact information, and a basic address/location. The owner can start working immediately.

After entry, show a quiet progress indicator for incomplete business setup. Legal details, VAT, SAPS licence, receipt settings, and other business data can be completed later.

Never require a new owner to know or configure Supabase manually.


8. SHOP IDENTITY / WHITE-LABEL EXPERIENCE
-----------------------------------------
Each shop should be able to configure: business name, logo, tagline, accent color, receipt header/footer, phone, WhatsApp/contact information, address, and other presentation settings.

The selected shop identity should appear throughout the app UI, login experience, receipts, contracts, printable item tags, and customer-facing outputs where appropriate.

LocalMarket branding remains in subtle platform locations such as login/support/about/version/footer areas, with Powered by LocalEats available as the platform attribution.

The database already contains shop_profiles and a metadata JSONB field; major branding settings should become explicit fields when that is cleaner and easier to query.


9. ACCOUNT CREATION AND LOGIN
-----------------------------
Owner login: email + password, with optional PIN for sensitive actions.

Staff login: the shop is remembered on the device after setup; staff use username + 6-digit PIN for fast entry. The UI should not force a cashier to type a shop code repeatedly.

Owner creates cashier/manager accounts from inside the app: Profile/Staff -> Add Cashier or Add Manager -> choose name, username, role, and 6-digit PIN -> create.

Cashiers should be able to remain active for up to 12 hours through a device/session policy. Show the session state and require re-authentication when the session expires.

Add Switch User and Lock Terminal controls in the profile/session area. Switching staff should not require signing out the whole terminal.

Forgot PIN: cashier sees a simple message telling them to ask the owner/manager to reset the PIN. The owner performs the reset inside Staff management.

Important backend correction: do not auto-assign new users to the first active shop. New users must be associated with the correct shop explicitly.


10. EXISTING STOCK FLOW
-----------------------
Existing Stock is a first-class acquisition path.

Flow: Add Stock -> Existing Stock -> Item Details -> Price -> Location -> Complete.

No Seller record. No Customer record. No SellerTransaction. No PawnLoan. No invented owner.

Fields: title, category, brand, model, serial/IMEI where applicable, condition, photo(s), retail price, optional cost basis, stock location, optional internal note, optional source note.

Provenance should support Existing Stock / Unknown / Not recorded. Honest unknown values are preferred to fake historical data.

Every item receives an automatic LocalMarket SKU/asset tag such as LM-000184.


11. BUY FROM PERSON FLOW
------------------------
Flow: Add Stock -> Buy From Person -> Seller Identification -> Item Details -> Valuation -> Deal Review -> Complete.

When a seller enters their ID number, the system should search the current shop records. If a match exists, show the sellers name immediately so staff can click the correct record.

Search should also support seller name, mobile number, and previous transaction/item references where practical.

Existing seller selection should show a small history summary such as transaction count, last item, last transaction date, and past amounts as role permits.

New seller creation uses the actual compliance information required for the business transaction. Do not mix sellers with pawn customers.


12. PAWN FLOW
-------------
Flow: Add Stock -> Pawn -> Customer Identification -> Item Details -> Loan Terms -> Pledge Review -> Complete.

For pawn, the customer must be a real persistent Customer record because the relationship continues through active loans, redemption, extensions, and history.

When a returning customer is found, show useful context: active loans, previous loans, outstanding amount, overdue state, and previous pawn activity, subject to permissions.

Compliance remains embedded in this workflow but should not make every screen feel like a police form.


13. RETAIL SALE / POS
---------------------
Retail buyers normally do not get persistent customer accounts. This avoids unnecessary Supabase rows and counter friction.

Core flow: Search or scan item -> Add to cart -> Payment -> Receipt -> Done.

Customer information is optional for cases such as requested SMS/WhatsApp receipt or warranty/service follow-up.

The POS should be a very fast cashier terminal. Search results should show photo, title, SKU, retail price, location, and availability so the correct item can be selected quickly.

Unique second-hand items generally use quantity 1. Generic stocked merchandise may support quantity greater than 1.


14. ITEM RECORD AND HISTORY
---------------------------
Clicking an item opens a detailed record with sections/tabs: Overview, Photos, History, Sale History, Acquisition, Compliance, Notes.

Cashier view: operational history such as added, price changed, moved, reserved, sold. Hide sensitive acquisition amounts, cost basis, seller payout, and profit.

Manager/owner view: full acquisition details, cost basis, margin/profit, seller, who changed what, timestamps, and audit details according to permission.

Every important state change should append a durable timeline event. Historical financial/compliance records must be preserved rather than overwritten.


15. ITEM PHOTOS
---------------
Photos are strongly encouraged and should be integrated directly into item entry.

Preferred minimum set: Front, Back, Serial/IMEI/Identification. Support 3 or more photos and allow additional photos when needed.

Show a visible documentation state such as 1/3 photos - Add photos or 3/3 photos. The UI should prompt users to add a picture without becoming annoying.

Image storage should use Backblaze B2; Supabase stores the image reference/storage key, not the binary image.


16. INVENTORY AND LOCATIONS
---------------------------
Inventory is the center of stock management.

Suggested simple locations: Retail Floor, Back Room, Vault, plus optional shelf/bin text such as Shelf B12. Do not create a complex warehouse system for the normal shop.

Inventory screen should support All Stock, Retail Floor, Secure Vault, Reserved, Flagged, and other relevant states without becoming visually crowded.

Add Stock must be a prominent but clean action from Inventory.


17. SEARCH AND DUPLICATE PROTECTION
-----------------------------------
Provide one global search that can find customer, seller, SKU, serial/IMEI, pawn ticket, receipt, and item name/description.

Results must clearly identify the result type: Inventory, Seller, Customer, Pawn Loan, Sale/Receipt.

For serialised items, detect duplicate serial/IMEI values within the shop and show a strong warning with the existing item before allowing continued entry when safe to do so.

For seller ID lookups, show the existing sellers name as soon as an exact match is found.


18. PRICE CONTROL
-----------------
Permanent retail price changes should be controlled by role.

Cashier may use an approved discount or controlled override workflow, but should not freely rewrite the permanent retail price.

Manager/owner can change the actual item price. The item history should record old price, new price, timestamp, and actor.

Cashiers should not see acquisition cost or profit unless a specific role permission allows it.


19. RETURNS, REFUNDS, AND VOIDING
---------------------------------
Recommendation: implement returns/refunds because real retail shops need them, but make them controlled operations.

Flow: find receipt -> select item(s) -> reason -> confirm -> inventory/cash update -> audit event.

Cashier should require manager approval for refunds/voids. Manager/owner can approve within permissions.

Every refund/void must be recorded in sale history and audit logs; do not delete the original transaction.


20. CASHIER SHIFTS / CASH CONTROL
---------------------------------
Implement a lightweight shift system.

Open Shift -> starting cash -> normal sales/cash movements -> Close Shift -> counted cash -> expected cash -> difference -> confirmation.

Show the cashier only what they need. Owner/manager can inspect shift reconciliation, differences, and cashier activity.

Cash differences should be visible as operational alerts, not silently ignored.


21. BULK INVENTORY IMPORT
-------------------------
Bulk import is essential for onboarding shops with existing stock.

Support CSV and XLSX import.

Template fields should include: SKU (optional), Title, Category, Brand, Model, Serial/IMEI, Condition, Cost, Retail Price, Location, Notes.

Allow default values for location, status, and category at the import level, with per-row overrides.

Process: upload -> parse -> validation/duplicate preview -> user review -> import -> summary.

Missing SKUs should be generated automatically. Invalid rows should be explained rather than silently dropped.


22. REPORTS
-----------
Keep first release reporting intentionally small.

Essential reports: Sales Report and Items Sold.

Provide Today, Yesterday, This Week, This Month, and Custom Date filters.

Useful fields: total sales, item count, average sale, sales by cashier, and individual transactions.

Advanced profitability/finance reporting can exist later but should not crowd the first-run experience.


23. COMPLIANCE
--------------
Compliance should operate behind the correct workflows.

Buy From Person and Pawn must preserve required statutory/compliance records and histories.

Existing Stock should not be forced into a fake seller/customer compliance record merely because it is being entered into the system after onboarding.

Compliance records are role-controlled and accessible from the relevant item/transaction history and manager/owner areas.


24. OFFLINE-FIRST OPERATION
---------------------------
Dexie/IndexedDB remains the local offline-first layer.

If internet is unavailable, permitted staff should still be able to sell, add existing stock, search local inventory, and perform permitted local workflows. Changes should queue for later sync.

When connectivity returns, sync should be entity-specific, idempotent, retryable, and should never claim success unless the remote write actually succeeded.

Supabase remains the shared business backend, while Dexie is the local operational database/cache/queue.


25. SUPABASE / MULTI-SHOP MODEL
-------------------------------
One owner account may eventually manage multiple shops/branches, but a single-shop owner should only see My Shop by default.

When a second shop is added, expose a simple Switch Shop control. Do not make branch management visible unless the owner uses more than one branch.

Every shop business record should be associated with the correct shop_id. Cross-shop leakage must be prevented by RLS and application scoping.

Current schema already includes shop_id across core business tables. Strengthen user/shop provisioning so account creation never silently attaches a person to an unrelated shop.


26. BACKEND DATA MODEL
----------------------
Core tables: shop_profiles, profiles, shop_items, customers, sellers, seller_transactions, pawn_loans, sales, saps_entries, system_logs.

Existing Stock must use acquisition_type = Existing Stock (or equivalent normalized representation) and must not populate seller_id/customer_id/pawn_ticket_id unless genuinely applicable.

Seller is separate from Customer. SellerTransaction is separate from Sale and PawnLoan.

Use stable UUIDs and idempotent sync. Preserve historical records.

RLS must ensure a user can access only records belonging to their shop/branch according to role.


27. SECURITY AND AUTHENTICATION RULES
-------------------------------------
Never expose Supabase secret/service-role credentials in the browser.

PINs should not remain as plain sensitive values if a secure hashing/verification mechanism can be implemented on the server. Avoid hardcoded default PINs in production.

Sensitive owner/manager actions should require appropriate role checks and may require re-authentication or a manager PIN.

Audit security-sensitive operations such as staff creation, PIN reset, refunds, price changes, profile changes, and critical compliance actions.


28. VISUAL DESIGN SYSTEM
------------------------
Visual direction: D - Facebook-style breathing room + professional POS speed. Do not copy Facebook branding, graphics, or colors.

Light theme should be the default. Keep terracotta as the brand accent, not the page background.

Use spacious layouts, generous whitespace, comfortable section gaps, restrained border radius, subtle shadows, readable type, and normal title case for human-facing labels.

Semantic colors: App background #F5F6F8, primary surface #FFFFFF, secondary surface #F8F9FA, text #1F2937, secondary text #6B7280, border #E5E7EB, brand #C85A32, brand hover #A94725, brand soft #FDF0EA, success #15803D, warning #B45309, danger #DC2626, info #2563EB.

The current repo has already transitioned to a light theme in commit 31ef614; continue refining consistency rather than reintroducing the old dark terminal aesthetic.


29. UX PSYCHOLOGY / HUMAN FACTORS
---------------------------------
The software should feel like a helpful coworker, not a bureaucratic obstacle.

Primary actions should be obvious without training. A trained cashier should know what to press. Quiet contextual guidance is allowed where it helps a new worker, but avoid tutorial walls, repeated popups, or excessive instructions.

Warnings should scale with importance: missing photo can warn and allow continuation; duplicate IMEI should be a strong warning; pawn without a customer must block completion; cash mismatch should require manager action.

Use clear language such as Add Stock, Sell, Buy From Person, Pawn, Search previous sellers, and Add photo. Avoid overly technical or statutory language on normal operational screens.


30. HARDWARE AND SCANNING
-------------------------
Scanning should be contextual. Sell can scan an item/SKU; Add Stock can scan/enter serial/IMEI; Inventory can scan to locate an item.

Global scanner access should remain removed from the main header.

Hardware integrations should fail honestly and should not present fake scan results or fake verification.


31. OWNER EXPERIENCE
--------------------
Owner dashboard: Sales Today, Items Sold Today, Stock Count, Active Pawns, Loans Due/Overdue, Recent Staff Activity, Alerts.

Owner settings: Business Profile, Branding, Staff, Reports, Shift Oversight, Compliance/Legal setup, and advanced configuration as needed.

Owner should be able to start using the application before completing every legal field. Incomplete setup should be visible but non-blocking unless a specific transaction legally requires the missing information.


32. CASHIER EXPERIENCE
----------------------
Cashier home: Sell, Add Stock, Find Item, Find Customer, small activity list.

Cashier should not be burdened with shop setup, legal configuration, business rules, deep reporting, sensitive cost data, or technical sync controls.

Cashier should be able to switch user/lock terminal and maintain a fast 12-hour operational session policy.


33. MANAGER EXPERIENCE
----------------------
Manager has operational oversight and approval power without exposing platform-level administration.

Manager can review/refund/void sales, control permanent price changes, inspect shifts, view operational reports, and handle permitted customer/seller/compliance tasks.

Owner remains the final authority for staff, branding, business configuration, and multi-shop control.


34. TECHNICAL CLEANUP REQUIRED BEFORE PRODUCTION
------------------------------------------------
Inspect and remove or quarantine legacy screens/components that are no longer part of the normal navigation, especially duplicate intake implementations.

Current repository contains both BuyPawn.tsx and IntakeDesk.tsx. App.tsx currently routes buy-pawn to BuyPawn; IntakeDesk should not become an accidental second source of truth.

Current AppContext still exposes several compatibility/stub methods. These should be reconciled with real domain contexts before production so the app does not silently present non-functional actions.

Profile provisioning, shop selection, RLS, sync, and staff account creation are production-critical and should be treated as architecture work rather than UI-only changes.


35. DEVELOPMENT RULES FOR AI STUDIO
-----------------------------------
Always inspect the existing repository before changing it.

Use the master product specification as the source of truth for product decisions.

Do not rebuild the app from scratch and do not redesign unrelated features.

Preserve working domain logic and improve it in place.

Never fabricate data, verification, sync success, or upload success.

After implementation, run TypeScript/build/lint where configured, test the primary workflows, and report exactly what changed and what remains.


36. PRIMARY ACCEPTANCE FLOWS
----------------------------
Flow A - New shop: owner creates account -> enters basic shop profile/logo -> enters dashboard -> can add existing stock immediately -> completes legal profile later.

Flow B - Existing stock: Add Stock -> Existing Stock -> item -> photo reminder -> price -> location -> SKU generated -> saved.

Flow C - Seller buy: Add Stock -> Buy From Person -> enter seller ID -> existing seller name appears if found -> select -> item -> photo(s) -> valuation -> deal -> seller transaction + compliance record -> inventory.

Flow D - Pawn: Add Stock -> Pawn -> enter customer ID -> existing customer appears if found -> select/create -> item -> photo(s) -> loan terms -> pledge -> customer + loan + vault/compliance records.

Flow E - Retail sale: Sell -> search/scan item -> cart -> payment -> receipt -> inventory marked sold -> sale recorded. No customer account required by default.

Flow F - Staff: Owner -> Staff -> Add Cashier -> username + 6-digit PIN -> cashier logs into remembered shop -> 12-hour session -> switch/lock user.

Flow G - Bulk import: Owner/manager -> Inventory -> Import Stock -> XLSX/CSV -> preview/validate -> fix/review -> import -> generated SKUs -> summary.


37. NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES
------------------------
Never force a person record when it is not part of the business event.

Never treat a retail buyer as a persistent customer by default.

Never treat an outright seller as a pawn customer.

Never treat Existing Stock as a SellerTransaction.

Never fabricate missing history.

Never expose sensitive cost/profit data to unauthorized roles.

Never make normal staff configure Supabase.

Never make the owner configure platform internals just to use the app.

Never claim synchronization, image upload, verification, or compliance actions succeeded when they did not.

Always optimize for speed, clarity, and low cognitive load.


38. FUTURE-READY FEATURES (NOT FIRST PRIORITY)
----------------------------------------------
Advanced profit/margin analytics, richer branch reporting, automated supplier management, deeper hardware integrations, customer communications, expanded document/contract workflows, and more advanced warehouse functions can be added later.

These must not be allowed to clutter the core counter workflow.

41. INTELLIGENT SOUTH AFRICAN-AWARE ASSISTANCE

---

LocalMarket should behave like a helpful coworker: recognize common inputs, reduce unnecessary typing, notice likely mistakes, and guide the worker to the next useful action without taking control away from them.

The goal is not to add "AI" for appearance. Intelligent behavior is valuable only when it removes work, prevents mistakes, or helps the worker finish the task faster.

41.1 SOUTH AFRICAN FORMATS

Where applicable, LocalMarket should recognize and normalize South African conventions for:

* Mobile phone numbers
* Landline phone numbers
* South African ID numbers
* Serial numbers
* IMEI numbers
* Retail/barcode identifiers
* Common contact formats
* Other structured identifiers commonly used by South African shops

Equivalent phone formats should be recognized as the same underlying number where appropriate.

Example:

067 205 4752
0672054752
+27 67 205 4752
+27672054752

These should be recognized as the same phone number for matching purposes when they represent the same number.

Store a normalized representation for matching while keeping a sensible human-readable display format.

Do not make the worker manually convert numbers between formats unless necessary.

41.2 VALIDATION STATES

Validation should distinguish between:

* Valid
* Suspicious
* Clearly invalid
* Already known / duplicate
* Unable to verify

A structurally valid number must not automatically be described as belonging to a real person.

For example, LocalMarket may determine that a phone number is structurally valid, but it must not claim that the number belongs to a particular person unless an appropriate verification source actually confirms this.

"Fake number" detection should mean that the value appears impossible, malformed, suspicious, duplicated, or unsuitable for the current business operation.

Never invent verification results.

41.3 SOUTH AFRICAN ID VALIDATION

South African ID numbers must be validated using appropriate structural rules and checksum validation.

Validation should happen consistently whether the ID is:

* Typed manually
* Pasted
* Scanned
* Retrieved from an existing record
* Entered during Buy From Person
* Entered during Pawn

A scanner must never bypass validation.

If an ID is invalid, LocalMarket should explain the problem in simple human language and take the worker directly to the ID field.

Do not simply display a generic:

"Validation failed."

Instead provide useful guidance such as:

"That ID number doesn't appear to be valid. Please check the number."

The exact technical validation reason may be available to managers/developers but should not clutter the normal cashier experience.

41.4 BARCODE AND SCANNER INTELLIGENCE

LocalMarket should recognize barcode input contextually.

The worker should not have to repeatedly tell the system:

"Now I am scanning a barcode."

The workflow should already know what type of identifier is expected.

Examples:

Sell:
Scan item/SKU -> find item -> add to cart.

Inventory:
Scan SKU/item tag -> open item.

Add Stock:
Scan serial/IMEI/barcode where applicable -> populate the relevant field.

If a barcode cannot be read or cannot be found:

* Do not pretend it was successfully scanned.
* Do not create a fake item.
* Offer the next useful action.
* Allow manual search or entry where permitted.

Scanner failures should be recoverable without forcing the worker to restart the entire workflow.

42. SUGGESTIONS WITHOUT FORCING

---

LocalMarket should provide intelligent suggestions when the worker types item information.

Possible suggestions include:

* Spelling corrections
* Product name corrections
* Brand suggestions
* Category suggestions
* Model suggestions
* Common terminology
* Formatting corrections
* Previously used shop terminology

Example:

Worker enters:

"samsng galaxy s24"

LocalMarket may show:

"Did you mean Samsung Galaxy S24?"

The worker can:

* Accept the suggestion
* Ignore the suggestion
* Continue using the original text

The system must never silently replace deliberate user input merely because it thinks another value is better.

The worker remains in control unless a specific business, security, compliance, or data-integrity rule requires validation before completion.

42.1 SUGGESTIONS SHOULD BE QUIET

Suggestions should assist rather than interrupt.

Avoid:

* Repeated popup messages
* Tutorial walls
* Constant correction dialogs
* Blocking the worker for harmless spelling mistakes
* Forcing category selection when the system can reasonably infer it

Prefer:

* Inline suggestions
* Small contextual prompts
* One-click acceptance
* Remembering accepted terminology
* Suggestions that disappear naturally when ignored

The worker should feel helped, not corrected by a teacher.

43. ERROR GUIDANCE — TAKE THE USER TO THE PROBLEM

---

An error notification by itself is not sufficient when the application knows exactly where the problem is.

When validation finds an error, LocalMarket should:

1. Identify the exact field, item, section, or step containing the problem.
2. Move focus to that location when practical.
3. Scroll the relevant field into view when necessary.
4. Clearly explain what needs attention.
5. Preserve all other information already entered.
6. Allow the worker to fix the problem immediately.
7. Revalidate the corrected value.
8. Continue to the next unresolved problem if more errors remain.

The worker should never have to hunt through a long form to discover what caused the error.

43.1 MULTIPLE ERRORS

When multiple problems exist, LocalMarket should guide the worker through them sequentially.

Example:

"There are 3 things to fix."

1 of 3 — ID number
2 of 3 — Purchase price
3 of 3 — Item photo

The system should take the worker directly to the first unresolved problem.

After it is corrected, move to the next unresolved problem.

After all problems are corrected, return the worker naturally to the completion action.

Do not repeatedly show the same error after it has been corrected.

43.2 PRESERVE USER WORK

Validation must not destroy the worker's existing input.

If an item form contains:

* Item name
* Brand
* Model
* Serial number
* Purchase price
* Retail price
* Notes
* Photos

and only the purchase price is invalid, the system must preserve everything else.

Never make the worker start the entire form again because one field is wrong.

43.3 ERROR LANGUAGE

Normal workers should receive human language rather than database or programming terminology.

Avoid:

"NULL constraint violation."

Prefer:

"Please enter the purchase price."

Avoid:

"PGRST303."

Prefer:

"Your session needs to reconnect. Your work is still here."

Avoid:

"Invalid UUID."

Prefer:

"We couldn't recognize that record. Please check the selected person."

44. WORK CONTINUITY / RESUMABLE DRAFTS

---

LocalMarket must preserve useful in-progress work across ordinary interruptions.

The experience should resemble a messaging application that remembers an unfinished conversation.

If the worker is interrupted, LocalMarket should remember what they were doing and allow them to continue without re-entering everything.

Interruptions include:

* Browser refresh
* Application restart
* PC power loss
* Temporary application crash
* Internet loss
* Terminal lock
* Staff account switch
* Closing and reopening the application
* Temporary navigation away from a workflow

44.1 EXAMPLES

If the worker was adding:

"Samsung Galaxy S24"

and the application closes, the next time they return LocalMarket may show:

"Welcome back. You were adding a Samsung Galaxy S24."

Buttons:

[Continue]

[Start something else]

If the worker was halfway through a Buy From Person transaction:

"Welcome back. You have an unfinished stock purchase."

[Continue]

[Discard]

If the worker was halfway through a Pawn transaction:

"Welcome back. You have an unfinished pawn transaction."

[Continue]

[Discard]

44.2 DRAFT STATE

A resumable draft may contain:

* Current workflow
* Current step
* Item information already entered
* Selected seller/customer
* Scanned identifiers
* Proposed price
* Retail price
* Condition
* Location
* Notes
* Validation state
* Photo references where safely supported
* Last meaningful screen
* Last meaningful action
* Timestamp of last activity

The draft should be stored locally using the offline-first architecture where safe and appropriate.

44.3 DRAFTS ARE NOT TRANSACTIONS

This distinction is critical.

An unfinished draft is NOT a completed business transaction.

Saving a draft must not:

* Complete a sale
* Pay a seller
* Create a completed pawn loan
* Release a pawn
* Mark an item permanently sold
* Create a final financial transaction
* Create compliance records that imply the transaction actually occurred
* Generate a false transaction history

Final business effects occur only when the actual completion workflow succeeds.

44.4 SENSITIVE INFORMATION

Resumable work must respect security and privacy.

If another employee switches accounts, the new employee must not automatically receive access to sensitive unfinished information belonging to the previous employee.

Sensitive drafts should be associated with the appropriate shop/user/session context.

The system may require the original worker to re-authenticate before continuing sensitive work.

Never expose another customer's private information merely because the previous employee left an unfinished workflow open.

44.5 OFFLINE DRAFTS

Draft continuity must work offline.

If the internet disappears while the worker is halfway through a workflow:

* Keep the work locally.
* Clearly show that LocalMarket is offline.
* Do not discard the draft.
* Do not require internet merely to preserve the draft.
* Sync appropriate completed data later when connectivity returns.

The worker should never have to remember what they typed just because the internet went down.

45. CURRENT WORK CONTEXT

---

LocalMarket should remember the worker's current context, not only completed database records.

Useful context includes:

* What the worker was doing
* Which item they were working on
* Which customer/seller they selected
* Which workflow they were in
* What step they reached
* What needs attention
* Whether the workflow is complete
* Whether information is waiting for synchronization

45.1 CONTEXTUAL RETURN

When the worker returns to LocalMarket, the system should prefer useful continuity over starting from a blank screen.

For example:

"Welcome back."

"You were adding Samsung Galaxy S24."

"2 things still need attention."

[Continue]

This should feel natural and calm.

Do not force a long explanation of what happened.

46. OFFLINE-FIRST HUMAN EXPERIENCE

---

Offline operation is a core product requirement.

Workers should know when LocalMarket is offline, but the application should not unnecessarily stop normal permitted work.

Example:

"Working offline — changes will sync automatically when you're back online."

The application should handle synchronization automatically.

Workers should not be expected to understand:

* Sync queues
* UUID reconciliation
* RPCs
* JWT refresh
* Database conflicts
* Retry logic
* IndexedDB
* Supabase
* Background synchronization

These are system responsibilities.

46.1 HONEST OFFLINE STATUS

LocalMarket must never claim:

"Synced"

unless the server actually accepted the relevant change.

Use clear states such as:

* Saved on this computer
* Waiting to sync
* Syncing
* Synced
* Needs attention

When a real conflict occurs, explain what the worker needs to do rather than exposing technical synchronization details.

47. ASSISTANT-LIKE UX PRINCIPLES

---

LocalMarket should feel like a helpful coworker.

The guiding principle is:

"Don't make the worker operate the software. Make the software assist the worker."

LocalMarket should:

* Remember what the worker was doing.
* Recognize common South African input formats.
* Reduce unnecessary typing.
* Recognize identifiers where possible.
* Detect likely mistakes.
* Suggest corrections without forcing them.
* Move the worker directly to errors.
* Preserve entered information.
* Preserve unfinished work after interruptions.
* Work offline.
* Explain offline state clearly.
* Hide database complexity.
* Hide unnecessary legal complexity.
* Prevent mistakes before they become transactions.
* Use the simplest useful next action.
* Never invent missing facts.
* Never pretend verification succeeded.
* Never pretend synchronization succeeded.
* Never bypass authorization or compliance rules.

47.1 THE TARGET FEELING

The target feeling for a normal shop worker is:

"I know what to do."

and ideally:

"Thank God this app makes this easier."

The worker should feel that LocalMarket is helping them complete their real job faster rather than giving them another job of operating complicated software.

48. INTELLIGENT WORKFLOW DECISION RULE

---

Before adding a new workflow step, AI Studio/developers should ask:

1. Does the worker actually need to provide this information?
2. Can LocalMarket determine it automatically?
3. Can LocalMarket remember it from previous work?
4. Can the system recognize the input without asking the worker to select a mode?
5. Can the system use South African formatting and conventions?
6. Can a suggestion replace unnecessary typing without forcing a correction?
7. If something is wrong, can the application take the worker directly to it?
8. Can the worker's existing work survive interruption?
9. Can the workflow continue safely offline?
10. Does the worker understand what is happening?
11. Is the worker protected from creating an accidental or invalid transaction?
12. Does the feature genuinely save time?

If a step does not provide meaningful value, remove it.

The default question should be:

"How can LocalMarket do this work for the worker?"

49. DEVELOPMENT ACCEPTANCE RULES FOR INTELLIGENT UX

---

Any new intelligent-assistance feature should be tested against the following:

Input recognition:

* Normal South African inputs are recognized.
* Equivalent formats are matched correctly.
* Invalid inputs are rejected appropriately.
* Suspicious inputs receive useful warnings.
* The system does not claim verification it did not perform.

Suggestions:

* Suggestions are optional.
* Original input is preserved until the worker accepts a correction.
* Suggestions do not interrupt normal work unnecessarily.

Errors:

* The exact problem is identified.
* Focus/navigation moves to the problem.
* Existing valid input is preserved.
* Multiple errors can be corrected sequentially.
* Completion cannot occur while blocking errors remain.

Continuity:

* Refresh does not unnecessarily destroy work.
* Application restart does not unnecessarily destroy work.
* Power loss does not unnecessarily destroy work.
* Network loss does not destroy drafts.
* Staff switching does not expose another employee's sensitive draft.
* Drafts cannot accidentally become completed transactions.

Offline:

* Worker knows they are offline.
* Permitted work continues.
* Local work is preserved.
* Synchronization is automatic where possible.
* The UI never falsely claims server synchronization.

Security:

* Role permissions remain enforced.
* Shop isolation remains enforced.
* Server authority remains authoritative.
* Sensitive information remains protected.
* Compliance workflows cannot be bypassed by intelligent assistance.

Performance:

* Intelligent assistance must make the worker faster.
* Do not introduce unnecessary network requests for simple local validation.
* Frequently used validation and suggestions should work offline where practical.
* Do not make a cashier wait for an AI service to perform something that can be determined locally.

The purpose of intelligent assistance is not to make LocalMarket look sophisticated.

The purpose is to make the shop worker's day easier.
